This Cookie Policy describes cookies, browser storage and similar technologies that may be used when you visit CyberoPlus. It complements our Privacy Policy, which explains broader personal-data practices.

1. What are cookies?

Cookies are small pieces of information that a website or third-party service can place in or read from your browser. Some expire when a browsing session ends, while others remain for a defined period or until removed. Similar technologies include browser local storage, identifiers, pixels and network requests used for functionality, security, advertising or measurement.

2. First-party and third-party cookies

First-party cookies are associated with the website you are visiting. Third-party cookies are associated with an external service integrated into that website, for example an advertising or platform provider. Modern browser rules can restrict third-party storage differently across devices and regions.

4. Google AdSense advertising cookies

CyberoPlus has Google AdSense integration. Google and other third-party vendors may place and read cookies or use web beacons, IP addresses and other identifiers when ads are served. Google uses advertising technologies to select and measure ads, manage frequency and help protect against fraud. Personalized ads, where permitted, may take previous visits to this or other websites into account.

Learn more in How AdSense uses cookies and How Google uses data from partner sites. To manage Google ad personalization, visit Google Ads Settings.

5. Blogger and Google platform technologies

Blogger and related Google services may process technical requests and use storage or identifiers to provide the publishing platform, maintain security and operate its features. Google's own Privacy Policy provides information about those services and their controls.

6. Measurement and local storage

Some website features or third-party integrations may use local storage rather than cookies, or collect technical performance events. If an analytics integration is active, it may process pages viewed, browser information and interaction data. Do not assume every optional feature or measurement tool is enabled for every visitor.

7. Managing consent

Where consent is needed, an applicable service or region-specific privacy message may offer choices about permitted purposes or vendors. Available controls depend on the actual configuration and on rules relevant to your location. The absence of a visible popup is not a statement that all tracking is necessary or that consent has been obtained.

If a privacy message provides a way to review or change preferences, use that control. If no such interface is offered to you, you can still manage browser storage and Google advertising preferences as described below. Browser settings are not necessarily a replacement for any consent requirements that a publisher must meet.

8. EEA, United Kingdom and Switzerland

Google has additional transparency and consent requirements for advertising to visitors in the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland, including certified consent-management platform requirements for relevant personalized advertising. These requirements depend on the advertising products and processing involved. Read Google's publisher consent guidance for details; this policy does not claim that a particular consent interface has been audited or certified for the site.

9. How to control cookies in your browser

  1. Open your browser's privacy or site settings.
  2. Review stored cookies and local site data for this domain and any associated third-party services.
  3. Delete selected entries or restrict cookies according to your preferences.
  4. Review Google Ads Settings for controls relating to Google's personalized advertising.

Deleting cookies can clear preferences or require you to make certain choices again. Restricting storage may affect functions such as contact widgets, embedded media or other integrated services.

10. Retention periods

Cookie and local-storage lifetimes differ by purpose, provider and configuration. Session cookies may be short-lived; others may remain until expiration or deletion. For cookies or identifiers controlled by Google or another provider, consult the provider's policy and settings. We do not provide invented universal expiration dates for cookies that may change independently.
